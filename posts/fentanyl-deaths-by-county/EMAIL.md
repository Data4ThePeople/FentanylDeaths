## 3. Body, part one

```
Last night we published a map of substance use treatment in every state. While building it, we looked at which drugs were behind the rise in overdose deaths. One drug stood out.

Fentanyl.

From 2013 to 2022, yearly overdose deaths in the U.S. went up by 63,959. Over the same years, deaths involving fentanyl went up by 70,733. Overdose deaths that did not involve fentanyl went down by 6,774.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Then in 2024, overdose deaths fell sharply, and fentanyl was the story again. Deaths involving fentanyl fell by 25,041, or 98% of the drop.

Today we are publishing a free, interactive map of fentanyl deaths in every U.S. county for every year from 1999 to 2024. Press play and watch it spread: in 2013, 55 counties had 10 or more fentanyl deaths. By 2022 there were 991.

The post also covers how fentanyl came to America, the southern West Virginia counties with the highest death rates on record, and the 20 counties where deaths went up in 2024.

What worries us is the unknown. Deaths fell fast in 2024, but the people who study it do not agree on why. If the drop came mostly from changes in the drug supply, it could reverse if the supply changes again. We walk through what the research says, and what it means for the programs whose funding is being decided now.
```
